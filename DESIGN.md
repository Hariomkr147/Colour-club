# Colour Club design direction

Working product name: Colour Club. Original visual identity, not a Jai Club clone.

Current direction, 10 November 2026: an adult gaming portal with a charcoal/forest table, pale jade action colour, clear point accounting and tactile number pieces. ENERGY 2 / RHYTHM 2 / MOTION 2. The player chooses a selection, then confirms in the adjacent slip. Administration shares the palette but uses compact operational tables.

- The low-luminance table keeps the countdown and selection controls distinct during repeated rounds, rather than using dark colours as a technology motif.
- The three static hero balls represent the actual number/colour mapping; they are decorative, hidden from assistive technology, and make no result claim.
- One short result-entry animation marks new results. Dialog entry and control transitions support interaction; reduced-motion disables them. No perpetual animation is used.
- A restrained material highlight on number pieces gives depth; no broad blue-purple gradient, glass or glow is used.
- Outcome explanation stays in the rules, acknowledgement and prediction slip. Public calculation controls and experimental language have been removed at the user's request. Detailed calculations stay in administrator records.

The earlier direction below is retained as historical design context, superseded by the current direction above.

- Warm ivory and ink create the feel of a printed game ticket, without a casino-style dark backdrop.
- Green, red and violet are semantic game colours; each is paired with a text label and number grouping.
- System rounded sans headings and Segoe UI body keep the interface readable and self-contained, with no font downloads.
- The oversized round countdown is the focal point because the time to place a prediction is the immediate decision.
- A broad game board and narrow prediction slip express the choose-then-confirm flow; the slip moves below the board on mobile.
- Number circles repeat in the board, recent results and history, giving the product one consistent game-piece motif.
- Solid backgrounds, hairline dividers and a single restrained ticket shadow establish grouping without glass or glow.
- A yellow ticket panel highlights the point balance, not invented revenue or player activity.
- Controls have visible keyboard focus, minimum 44px targets, explicit selection text, and no continuous animation.
- A full-width disclosure and acknowledgement in registration explain the house-controlled outcome before play.

UI UX Pro Max search was run. Its automated retro-futurist/CRT recommendation was rejected because scanlines, flashing effects and childlike typography do not fit this readable adult free-play board. Accessibility and responsive recommendations were retained.

Appearance review takes place after the initial implementation, per the user's selected workflow. No external illustrations, profile photographs or logo assets are needed.
